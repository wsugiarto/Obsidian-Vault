### I. Introduction to Volcanoes

**Key Terms:**

- **Volcanology:** The science of volcanic phenomena; a sub-set of geology.
- **Volcano:** A place where volcanic materials emerge onto Earth’s surface in some fashion.
- **Eruption:** An event in which magma emerges from the Earth’s surface.
- **Lava vs. Magma:** **Magma** is molten rock _inside_ the planet. **Lava** is molten rock on the _surface_ of Earth.

**Why we study volcanoes:** To understand Earth's interior and evolution, to mitigate geologic hazards, for agriculture (fertile soils), energy exploration (geothermal), mineral exploration (especially metals), and to understand the impact of volcanism on climate and the atmosphere.

**"Four Classic Eruptions":** _Note: The specific text of the "Four classic eruptions" assigned reading is not in the sources. However, the slides highlight specific volcanoes that serve as primary examples for different eruption styles:_

- **Paricutín (Mexico):** A scoria (cinder) cone that grew from a cornfield in 1943. It is a monogenetic volcano (erupted once and shut off).
- **Mt. Vesuvius (Italy, 79 A.D.):** The type example for **Plinian** eruptions, which are violent and produce vertical columns of gas and ash up to 55 km.
- **Mt. Pelée (Martinique, 1902):** The type example for **Peleean** eruptions, associated with lava domes and pyroclastic flows (block and ash flows) caused by dome collapse.
- **Mauna Loa (Hawaii):** A massive shield volcano characterized by broad, passive lava flows.

### II. General Geology

**Earth Facts:**

- **Layers:** The Earth is layered into the Iron Core (solid inner, liquid outer), the Mantle, and the Crust (0-40 km).
- **Age:** The Earth is 4.54 billion years old.

**Plate Tectonics:**

- **Basic Idea:** The Earth’s crust is divided into plates that move. The "engine" driving this movement involves magma, mantle convection, and the concept of **seafloor spreading** (upwelling mantle erupts at ridges, moves away, and recycles back into the mantle).
- **3 Types of Boundaries:**
    1. **Divergent:** Plates move **apart** (arrows pointing away). Associated with **Mid-ocean ridges** where new crust is created.
    2. **Convergent:** Plates move **together** (arrows pointing toward each other). Associated with **Subduction** (where the denser plate sinks into the mantle) and mountain building. ~90% of volcanoes are located here.
    3. **Transform:** Plates move **side-to-side** (smearing). Associated with faults like the San Andreas.

**Rocks and Minerals:**

- **Mineral:** Naturally occurring, usually inorganic, solid, with an ordered internal structure and fixed chemical composition.
- **Rock:** An aggregate of (usually) multiple minerals.
- **Three Types of Rocks:**
    1. **Igneous:** "Made from fire." Crystallizes directly from magma or lava.
    2. **Sedimentary:** Composed of fragments/pieces of rock and mineral matter compacted into rock (lithified).
    3. **Metamorphic:** Earth materials subjected to stress (heat/pressure) that recrystallize.

### III. Magmas and Eruptions

**Magma Components:**

- **Magma:** Molten rock found within the Earth.
- **3 Components:** Melt (liquid phase), Solids (crystals), and Gas (dissolved volatiles).

**Volatiles (Gases):**

- **Common Volatiles:** Water vapor (H2O), Carbon dioxide (CO2), and Sulfur dioxide (SO2) are the "big 3." H2O makes up 70-90% of total gases.
- **Temperature Variation:** SO2 is associated with high temperatures; H2S (rotten egg smell) is associated with low temperatures.
- **Exsolution:** The process where gases come out of solution, form bubbles, and escape. This occurs because **pressure decreases** as magma rises toward the surface. This gas release **drives eruptions**.

**Magma Formation and Location:**

- Magma mainly forms in the **mantle** and by melting of the crust. It rises because it is less dense (buoyant).
- **Intrusive (Plutonic):** Rocks that cool **slowly below** the surface (e.g., Granite, Dikes).
- **Extrusive (Volcanic):** Rocks that cool **quickly at** the surface (e.g., Lava flows, Ash).

**Silica (SiO2) and Viscosity:**

- **Relationship:** Higher silica (SiO2) content means **higher viscosity** (stickier/thicker).
    - **Basalt:** ~50% SiO2 $\rightarrow$ Low viscosity (fluid).
    - **Andesite:** ~60% SiO2 $\rightarrow$ Medium viscosity.
    - **Rhyolite:** ~70% SiO2 $\rightarrow$ High viscosity (sticky).
- **Explosivity:** Determined by **viscosity** and **volatile content**.
    - High Viscosity + Volatiles = **Explosive** (Boom).
    - Low Viscosity + Volatiles = **Passive** (Lava flows).

### IV. Types of Volcanoes and Eruptions

**Three Primary Volcano Types:**

1. **Shield Volcano:** Wide/broad structures formed by passive, low-viscosity lava flows (e.g., Mauna Loa).
2. **Scoria (Cinder) Cone:** Piles of tephra formed by "monogenetic" eruptions (erupt once then shut off).
3. **Stratovolcano (Composite):** Tall, pointy mountains formed by multiple explosive eruptions and layers of tephra and lava (e.g., Mt. Fuji, Mt. Rainier).

**Eruption Styles (Mercalli Classification):**

- **Hawaiian:** Low intensity, passive, basaltic, fire fountains, spatter. Columns <2 km high.
- **Strombolian:** Short, rhythmic bursts of "blobs" and incandescent fragments. Considered "passive" but can be violent. Columns <10 km.
- **Vulcanian:** Short, explosive bursts of viscous magma. "Throat clearing" eruptions. Columns ~20 km.
- **Plinian:** Violent, continuous, sustained vertical columns of gas/ash up to 55 km (e.g., Vesuvius).
- **Peleean:** Associated with **lava domes** and dome collapse, generating pyroclastic flows.
- **Hydrovolcanic ("Wet"):** Eruptions driven by magma interacting with external water (groundwater, sea water).
    - **Surtseyan:** A specific type of wet eruption involving sea water/magma interaction.
    - Explosivity is determined by the **H2O : Melt ratio** (ratios between 0.1 and 0.3 are most explosive).

### V. Lava Flows

**Basaltic Lava Types:**

- **Pahoehoe:** Smooth, ropy texture. "Friendly to the foot." Moves slowly, often forms **lava tubes**.
- **A’a:** Fragmented, rubbly, rough surface. "Unfriendly to the foot." Moves faster, often forms channels/rivers.

**Lava Features:**

- **Fire Fountain:** Caused by the rapid expansion of volatiles (gas) driving molten rock into the air.
- **Spatter:** Blobs of molten rock that land and stick together.
- **Lava Tubes:** Form when the surface of a flow "crusts over" while liquid lava continues to flow underneath in a tunnel. This insulates the lava, allowing it to travel farther.
- **Skylights:** Formed from the collapse of the ceiling of a lava tube.
- **Lava Lake:** A pool of molten lava contained within a vent or crater (e.g., Erta Ale).
- **Columnar Jointing:** Cooling feature where lava constricts into hexagonal columns (e.g., Giant's Causeway).

**High Silica Lavas (Andesite/Dacite/Rhyolite):**

- **Blocky Lava Flow:** Typical of intermediate (Andesite) magmas; flows are thick with rubbly surfaces.
- **Lava Dome:** Formed by high-viscosity (sticky) magma (Dacite/Rhyolite) that piles up near the vent rather than flowing away.
- **Obsidian:** Volcanic glass formed from felsic (high silica) lava that cools without crystallizing.
